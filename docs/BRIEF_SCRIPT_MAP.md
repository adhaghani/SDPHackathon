# Script-to-visual map

Source: `borrowlah-3r-pitch-team-pack (1).pdf`, pages 2–6. All five narration sections are preserved word for word. Each Right / Page Down advances one beat; Left / Page Up reverses it. The opening is a pre-speech hold.

The original washer, chair and bottles appear in the named opening. Later generic examples use a drill and ladder, books and a plant, paper and cans. These are illustrative examples, not resident listings.

## 1. Member 1 — The planet

- **Beat 1:** Opening hold before narration. → A greener township starts with us. Everyday choices. Shared locally.

## 2. Member 1 — Three everyday situations

- **Beat 1:** A pressure washer used once. → Three everyday situations. A useful tool, needed for a single task.
- **Beat 2:** A chair nobody needs anymore. → Three everyday situations. Something useful, ready for a new home.
- **Beat 3:** Bottles waiting to be sorted. → Three everyday situations. Materials with somewhere better to go.
- **Beat 4:** Three everyday situations. Three opportunities to make a different choice for our planet. → Three chances to choose differently. For our planet. Starting close to home.

## 3. Member 1 — The three Rs

- **Beat 1:** Reduce what we buy unnecessarily. → Reduce. Borrow before buying for an occasional task.
- **Beat 2:** Reuse what still has value. → Reuse. Pass useful things to someone who needs them.
- **Beat 3:** Recycle what can be recovered. → Recycle. Sort materials and record contributions.

## 4. Member 1 — Meet BorrowLah

- **Beat 1:** We built BorrowLah to make these three actions easier to practise with the people living around us. → Three choices. One neighbourhood. BorrowLah brings the three Rs close to home.
- **Beat 2:** A resident can borrow instead of buying, → Borrow instead of buying. Use an item that already exists nearby.
- **Beat 3:** pass useful things to a neighbour, → Pass useful things on. A new home for something you no longer need.
- **Beat 4:** and record their recycling contributions. → Record your contribution. Make self-reported recycling participation visible.
- **Beat 5:** Our goal is to help townships use resources more carefully and make the three Rs part of everyday life. → Make the three Rs everyday choices. A goal to work towards, together.
- **Beat 6:** Let us show you how each R works in BorrowLah. → Let’s see the three Rs. Reduce → Reuse → Recycle

## 5. Member 2 — Reduce

- **Beat 1:** First, Reduce. → Need it once? Borrow it. Reduce unnecessary purchases.
- **Beat 2:** Before buying something for a single task, we can ask: could I borrow it nearby? → Could I borrow it nearby? An afternoon’s task. A neighbour’s useful tool.

## 6. Member 2 — Find, check, request

- **Beat 1:** In BorrowLah, a resident finds a pressure washer, → Find the right tool. Start with an existing item in the neighbourhood.
- **Beat 2:** checks its availability, → Check availability. Choose around the item’s other bookings.
- **Beat 3:** selects dates and sends a request. → Choose dates. Send a request. The owner reviews the request next.

## 7. Member 2 — Approve, use, return

- **Beat 1:** The owner approves. → The owner approves. Approval starts the arrangement.
- **Beat 2:** At collection, both neighbours confirm handover. → Both confirm collection. A shared record of the handover.
- **Beat 3:** After use, the borrower marks it returned, → Use it. Mark it returned. The owner still needs to confirm.
- **Beat 4:** and the owner confirms its condition and return. → The owner confirms return. Record the condition and confirm the return.
- **Beat 5:** That completes the loan and makes the item available to share again, subject to its other bookings. → Ready to share again. Loan completed. Other bookings still apply.

## 8. Member 2 — Why borrowing matters

- **Beat 1:** This gives residents a practical alternative to owning an item they only need occasionally. → Access for a task. An alternative to owning an occasional-use item.
- **Beat 2:** The environmental aim is to avoid unnecessary purchases by making better use of what already exists. → Use what already exists. Aim: avoid unnecessary purchases through sharing.
- **Beat 3:** Whether residents actually replace a purchase is something we must assess in a pilot. → Did borrowing replace a purchase? A question for the proposed pilot, not a proven outcome.

## 9. Member 3 — Reuse

- **Beat 1:** Second, Reuse. → Done with it? Pass it on. Useful things deserve another use.
- **Beat 2:** An item can stop being useful to one household while still being useful to another. → Same things. A different need. Things you no longer need could be useful to a neighbour.

## 10. Member 3 — Create a giveaway

- **Beat 1:** In Giveaway, a resident lists an item, → Give it a visible listing. Help neighbours discover something useful.
- **Beat 2:** describes its condition → Describe its condition. Make the item’s condition clear.
- **Beat 3:** and chooses a shared handover location. → Meet in a shared place. Choose a shared location for handover.

## 11. Member 3 — A neighbour claims it

- **Beat 1:** A neighbour selects Ask to take this. → “Ask to take this.” A neighbour finds something they can use.
- **Beat 2:** The item becomes reserved, → Reserved for its next owner. The handover comes next.

## 12. Member 3 — Both confirm handover

- **Beat 1:** and both people confirm when the handover happens. → Two people. A shared handover. Giver and recipient each confirm.
- **Beat 2:** Only after both confirmations is the giveaway completed. → Both confirmed. Giveaway completed. The items move to their next owner.
- **Beat 3:** This is different from borrowing: the item moves to a new owner, with no return expected. → A new owner. No return expected. A permanent transfer of a useful item.

## 13. Member 3 — Keep useful things in use

- **Beat 1:** The aim is to keep useful things in use before they become waste. → Keep useful things in use. Give that next use a place to begin.
- **Beat 2:** BorrowLah gives that next use a place to begin: a visible listing, → A visible listing. Make something useful discoverable.
- **Beat 3:** a neighbour who wants it, → Someone who can use it. Connect the item with its next owner.
- **Beat 4:** and a confirmed handover. → A confirmed new beginning. The next use starts with a completed handover.

## 14. Member 4 — Recycle

- **Beat 1:** Third, Recycle. → Sort it. Log it. Keep going. Paper, plastic, metal and glass.
- **Beat 2:** After sorting and taking materials for recycling, residents can record what they contributed. → Recycle first. Then record it. The log records a physical contribution.

## 15. Member 4 — Two ways to record

- **Beat 1:** In the recycling log, they can count common items → Count common items. Choose from the units available for each material.
- **Beat 2:** or enter an estimated weight by material. → Or enter estimated kg. Custom weight is an alternative to item counts.

## 16. Member 4 — From counts to estimates

- **Beat 1:** BorrowLah converts counts into estimated kilograms → Counts become estimated kg. Item count × weight per unit.
- **Beat 2:** and shows a material breakdown. → See it by material. A breakdown of the recorded contributions.

## 17. Member 4 — Documented factors

- **Beat 1:** Where supported by documented factors, it also shows estimated carbon and energy equivalents. → Estimates need a documented basis. Carbon and energy equivalents appear only where supported.

## 18. Member 4 — What the log tells us

- **Beat 1:** These are self-reported estimates, not independently measured collection weights. → Self-reported. Estimated. These are not independently measured collection weights.
- **Beat 2:** Logging an entry does not itself recycle the material. → A log records the action. The physical recycling happens outside the app.

## 19. Member 4 — A shared recycling goal

- **Beat 1:** Township teams can also set a shared recycling goal. → One township. A shared goal. Township teams can set a recycling campaign goal.
- **Beat 2:** When a campaign is active, residents can see collective progress and how their logs contribute. → See how your contribution fits. Collective progress is visible when a campaign is active.

## 20. Member 4 — Keep the three Rs going

- **Beat 1:** The purpose is to make recycling participation visible → Make participation visible. A record of residents’ recycling contributions.
- **Beat 2:** and encourage people to keep going, → Keep going. Together. Encourage continued participation.
- **Beat 3:** alongside reducing purchases and reusing useful things. → All three Rs work together. Reduce purchases. Reuse useful things. Recycle responsibly.

## 21. Member 5 — Three choices together

- **Beat 1:** BorrowLah brings these three choices together: borrow before buying, → Borrow before buying. Reduce unnecessary purchases.
- **Beat 2:** pass things on, → Pass things on. Reuse what still has value.
- **Beat 3:** and recycle responsibly. → Recycle responsibly. Sort materials and record contributions.

## 22. Member 5 — BetterPoints

- **Beat 1:** BetterPoints support participation through eligible completed activities → Recognise contributions. BetterPoints support eligible completed activities.
- **Beat 2:** and recycling campaign goals. → Shared goals can support participation. Recycling campaign rules and eligibility apply.
- **Beat 3:** They recognise contributions; they are not a measure of environmental impact. → Recognition needs context. BetterPoints recognise participation, not environmental impact.

## 23. Member 5 — A proposed local pilot

- **Beat 1:** For Sime Darby Property, we propose starting with one township and a small resident pilot across the three Rs. → Start with one township. Proposed: a small resident pilot across all three Rs.

## 24. Member 5 — What we would track

- **Beat 1:** We would track completed loans, → Completed loans. Track loans that reach a confirmed return.
- **Beat 2:** giveaway handovers, → Giveaway handovers. Track transfers confirmed by both people.
- **Beat 3:** recycling logs → Recycling logs. Keep self-reported contributions clearly labelled.
- **Beat 4:** and repeat participation, → Repeat participation. Do residents return to take another action?
- **Beat 5:** alongside resident feedback → Listen to residents. Understand what works and what needs to improve.
- **Beat 6:** and staff effort. → Understand staff effort. Consider the work required to run the pilot.

## 25. Member 5 — Learn before making claims

- **Beat 1:** That would help us understand what people use → Learn what people use. Participation tells us which features residents use.
- **Beat 2:** and what environmental evidence we still need to collect. → Find the evidence we still need. Environmental outcomes need their own evidence.

## 26. Member 5 — Closing

- **Beat 1:** Our goal is to make caring for the planet easier to practise close to home. → Caring for the planet starts close to home. Everyday choices. Shared locally.
- **Beat 2:** BorrowLah. Reduce. Reuse. Recycle. → Reduce. Reuse. Recycle. BorrowLah
- **Beat 3:** Share more. Connect more. Live better. → Share more. Connect more. Live better. BorrowLah · Reduce. Reuse. Recycle.
